3.8. *A delicate articulation with creditor protection mechanisms* The detailed study of the protection regime for both minority shareholders and workers also highlighted the fact that there is a real interdependence between all protection regimes. Indeed, the effectiveness of the creditors’ defensive system is intimately linked to the proportionality of the mechanisms for protecting minority shareholders and workers, which must neither lead to excessive financial erosion nor constitute a disproportionate obstacle to the completion of a transaction on which the ultimate satisfaction of creditors may depend. On the basis of this observation, it has thus been possible to identify certain aspects of the current regime of cross-border divisions by incorporation of new companies which would deserve to be improved in the framework of a new European initiative on cross-border mobility.
4. *General conclusion*
4.1. *The future of cross-border divisions* Complex and delicate, cross-border divisions give the impression of a fragile clockwork, and the main challenge of a larger-scale initiative in this area will be to preserve the clever balance between the different potentially conflicting interests in presence under the impetus of the ECJ’s progressive case law on freedom of establishment. At the end of this study, it appears that the best way to make cross-border divisions a reality within the European Union would be to proceed by means of a new directive on the subject, enshrining a seven-step procedure, the main elements of which would be derived from the regime for cross-border divisions by incorporation of new companies contained in the Mobility Directive, subject to a significant number of adaptations or corrections. The latter, identified in the course of the presentation, could be highlighted by means of a detailed analysis of the provisions of the Mobility Directive while taking full advantage of the lessons learned from (i) the ECJ case law on the freedom of establishment, (ii) the successful experience of cross-border merg- ers, (iii) the established regime of domestic divisions contained in the Company Law Directive and (iv) a broad comparative law exercise between the laws of Belgium, Luxembourg, Germany, Austria and France. However, cross-border divisions are not limited to majority shareholders controlling the process or members of the relevant management bodies but are also likely to affect a significant number of other stakeholders, mainly creditors, minority sharehold- ers and employees. The methodical examination of the risks generated by this type of transaction for each of these categories of stakeholders combined with the evaluation of the various protection mechanisms developed by both the European and national legislators, while taking into account the ECJ’s lessons in this area and the imperative need to strike a balance between the different interests involved lead to the conclusion that a revised regime for cross-border divisions will have to include a complex system of protection of *stakeholders*’ interests articulated around (i) an elaborate regime for the protection of credi- tors’ interests composed of four successive lines of defence progressively more intrusive from the point of view of freedom of establishment, (ii)  a two-tier
