Moreover, the analysis of both the regime of cross-border mergers before the entry into force of the Mobility Directive in the light of the ECJ’s lessons in the *KA Finanz* decision¹² and the new regime introduced by the Mobility Directive pleads against a reinforcement of the creditor protection mechanisms solely because of the cross-border nature of the transaction. While this conclusion is only par- tially supported by an examination of the position of both the Luxembourg and the Belgian legislators, and even if it is undeniable that the ECJ would be very reserved with regard to the conformity with freedom of establishment of such a violation of the principle of equivalence, it is impossible to conclude *ab initio* *that* such an impediment cannot be justified. On the other hand, the argument that increased protection of creditors impacted by a change in the *lex societatis* of their creditor was justified on account of the procedural difficulties which they would be facing in the event of a cross-border division could be refuted. It was further demonstrated that a cross-border division does not affect the law applicable to the contract into by and between a creditor and the divided com- pany. Furthermore, the argument that greater protection is justified because the national law applicable to the receiving companies is less protective than that of the divided company could not be upheld due to the partial harmonisation of the different national company laws by the European legislator on the one hand and its overly simplistic nature. Consequently, an effective creditor protection regime will necessarily have to be largely based on the regime applicable to domestic transactions and the cross-border nature of the transaction alone does not justify tilting the balance in favour of the interests of creditors. However, a divided company’s creditors will require particular attention in return for the great freedom enjoyed by the shareholders with regard to the distribution of the assets and liabilities of the divided entity combined with the substitution of the debtor which is imposed on them without their consent.
