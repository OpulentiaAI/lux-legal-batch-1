If this analy- sis has shown that the approach of the EU legislator in the Mobility Directive is much less restrictive than it appears at first sight and that its protection regime would to a very large extent be perfectly compatible with cross-border divisions involving more than one pre-existing company, it would be erroneous to con- sider that the protection regime enshrined in the Mobility Directive is purely and simply transposable. Indeed, many aspects would need to be reviewed to take better account of the imperatives of freedom of establishment and the protection of creditors’ interests. Moreover, the choice of the European legis- lator to opt for a resolutely minimal harmonisation regarding the protection of minority shareholders can only prove insufficient in the long term. However, it is possible to consider these legislative choices as a necessary precondition for making a more ambitious harmonisation politically acceptable.

3.6. *The challenges of protecting employee involvement* Reluctant to any harmonisation and politically very sensitive, the question of preserving employee involvement¹⁵ is a significant issue in a cross-border division as this type of operation could easily be used to avoid or undermine it.
