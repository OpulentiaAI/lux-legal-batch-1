4.2. *A clever move by the Commission?* Beyond this first conclusion, the in-depth examination of several provisions of the Mobility Directive made it possible to identify a significant number of provisions whose *ab initio* inadequacy reveals the need for a new European initiative in this area. To such extent that the future evaluation to be carried out by the European Commission by 1
st February 2027 18 will in fact leave few surprises as to its conclusion. It can therefore be seen as a clever political maneuver on the part of the European Commission which, by voluntarily select- ing the sticking points in the regime of cross-border divisions and by counting on a significant increase in the number of such operations through the at least partially harmonised legal framework of the Mobility Directive, hopes to make it easier for reluctant Member States to accept a new directive of a more extensive nature. Future developments in the law on the cross-border mobility of companies within the EU may make it possible to settle this question, but it will already be interesting to study the extent to which, when transposing the provisions of the Mobility Directive into national law, the Member States will naturally tend to remedy the imperfections of the current European regime for cross-border divisions.
