1. *Introduction* Benefiting from renewed interest, of which the *Polbud* decision¹, the Mobility Directive² and the recent initiative of the European Law Institute aimed at achieving a Rome IV Regulation on the law applicable to companies³ are only epiphenomena, the cross-border mobility of companies remains more than ever in the spotlight. However, it would be erroneous to analyse this trend as the manifestation of a unanimous desire to build an internal market where borders would gradually evaporate for companies. On the contrary, both the case law of the European Court of Justice on freedom of establishment and the slow and fragmentary construction of secondary European law on cross-border mobility illustrate perfectly to what extent any development in this area will necessarily be torn between the mistrust, or even protectionism, of certain Member States concerned by the impact of unbridled mobility on the various stakeholders and, on the contrary, the benevolence and liberalism of other Member States hop- ing for their national economies to benefit. It is at the heart of this dichotomy that this study on a virtually unexplored mechanism of cross-border mobility lies.
