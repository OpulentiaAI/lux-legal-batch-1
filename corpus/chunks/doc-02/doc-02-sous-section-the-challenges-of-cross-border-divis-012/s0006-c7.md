3. *Stakeholders’ perspective on cross-border divisions*
3.1. *A wider spectrum* Cross-border divisions, far from only affecting companies and their majority shareholders, are also likely to have a significant impact on the situation of other stakeholders, mainly creditors, minority shareholders and employees. It is therefore essential to broaden the scope of the analysis so as to adequately take into account the main challenges of a cross-border division for these various stakeholders as the implementation of an efficient regime within the European Union is inconceivable without adequate safeguards for their interests. The sec- ond part of the analysis will therefore (i) identify to what extent the protection mechanisms enshrined in the Mobility Directive would be compatible with the specificities of a cross-border division involving more than one pre-existing company and (ii) remedy to any shortcomings of the latter in this respect. The method used, although systematically requiring certain adaptations due to the specificities of each category of stakeholders, is based on three-steps approach, namely (i)  the identification of the challenges of a cross-border division for each category of stakeholder, (ii) the study of the way in which such challenges were addressed by the European legislator in the Mobility Directive and (iii) the determination of the necessary changes required in order to make any type of division a reality within the EU without unduly sacrificing the interests of the other stakeholders and without losing sight of the impact of freedom of establishment. The latter implies that any protection mechanism will neces- sarily have to respect both the principle of equivalence and the principle of effectiveness and any obstacle to the freedom of establishment generated by the establishment of specific protection mechanisms will necessarily have to comply with the conditions of the so-called “*Gebhard test*”.
3.2. *Challenges of an effective creditor protection regime* Fundamentally a heterogeneous category of stakeholder, a company’s credi- tors may incur up to six distinct risks as a result of a cross-border division. Among these, the double risk linked to the universal transfer of assets and liabilities, the risk linked to the cross-border nature of the transaction and the risk linked to the free allocation of the fractions of the assets and liabilities of the divided entity affect either exclusively or mainly the creditors of the divided company. As harmonisation of protection mechanisms at EU level is much preferable both in order to enhance the legal certainty of cross-border divisions and to reduce the associated costs, the first challenge of an effective creditor protec- tion regime will be to be sufficiently flexible to effectively adapt to the varying risks incurred by the various creditors impacted by the operation. The second challenge will be to develop a defensive strategy striking a delicate balance between the interests of creditors and those of the company and its majority shareholders.
