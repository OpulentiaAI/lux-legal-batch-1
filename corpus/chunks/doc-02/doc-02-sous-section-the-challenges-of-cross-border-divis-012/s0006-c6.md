2.8. *A latent reform* Beyond the mere demonstration of the compatibility of the European model for cross-border operations and the identification of the procedural aspects still requiring adaptation to the specific features of a cross-border division involving more than one pre-existing company, the detailed analysis of the regime of cross-border divisions by incorporation of new companies enshrined in the Mobility Directive also revealed a significant number of indi- cations of the Commission’s political skill. This applies in particular to (i)  the rule on the language of the draft terms of division which originally appeared in the Commission Proposal⁹, (ii) the obligation to indicate in the draft terms of division the date from which the shares of the recipient companies allocated in the course of the operation give the right to participate in the profits and (iii) the possibility to provide for a retroactive accounting effective date. In addi- tion, more fundamentally, the Commission could not ignore the fact that the decision of a competent national authority to refuse to issue the certificate of conformity in the context of a cross-border division by acquisition on the simple ground that this type of operation did not fall within the scope of the national provisions transposing the Mobility Directive would violate the principle of non-discrimination of cross-border operations vis-à-vis domestic ones laid down by the ECJ in the *Sevic* judgment¹⁰. This further strengthens the thesis that the Commission, through the Mobility Directive, did not only intended to harmonise cross-border divisions by incorporation of new companies, but already wished to prepare a more ambitious and thorough reform of divisions¹¹.
