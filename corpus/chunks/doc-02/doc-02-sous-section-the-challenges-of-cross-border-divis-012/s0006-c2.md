2.3. *The impact of fundamental freedoms* On the basis of this broad definition, it is possible to examine cross-bor- der divisions from the particular perspective of freedom of establishment and free movement of capital. Such an exercise will result in identifying three dif- ferent lessons from the ECJ case law in this area which will lay the founda- tions for a European regime for cross-border divisions. Firstly, the detailed study of the ECJ lessons with respect to cross-border mergers, transfers of seat and cross-border conversions makes it possible to establish in particular that cross-border divisions constitute “particular methods of exercise of the freedom of establishment”
4. Consequently, the principles of equivalence and effectiveness imply that both the home and host Member States (provided they are familiar with national divisions) can neither treat cross-border divisions less favourably than domestic operations, nor make them impossible in practice or excessively difficult through their national legislation. Second, any barrier to freedom of establishment will only be permissible if it meets the conditions of the so-called “*Gebhard test*” 5. In this respect, the fight against abuse of the freedom of establishment, the preservation of the effectiveness of tax controls or the fairness of commercial transactions and the protection of the interests of creditors, minority shareholders, employees or the tax authorities constitute overriding reasons in the general interest likely to justify such a barrier. Thirdly and from analysing the ECJ case law, the conflict of laws rule applicable to cross-border divisions by incorporation of new companies should lead to the application of the law of the Member State of the company being divided for the part of the procedure up to the issue of the pre-division certificate, whereas the rest of the procedure should in principle be governed by the law of the Member States of the new companies. In addition, the absence of secondary legislation specifically governing cross-border divisions cannot justify a refusal by Member States to recognise this type of operation if national divisions are allowed.
